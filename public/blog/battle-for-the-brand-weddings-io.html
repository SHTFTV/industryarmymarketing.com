Act I — The land grab nobody made (2013–2015)

For two decades, every legacy wedding directory parked itself on a .com and called it a brand. weddings.com went stale. theknot.com turned into a paid-listing arms race. Meanwhile the cleanest, most brandable category name in the entire industry — weddings.io — sat unclaimed.

We filed it on May 13, 2015. The Internet Archive had already started capturing the domain earlier as a placeholder; today the Wayback Machine shows 78 captures between May 17, 2013 and January 16, 2026.2 That public timeline is the receipts.

"The .io was sitting on the shelf. Every legacy player walked past it for fifteen years. We picked it up, and the battle started the same week."

Act II — The quiet build (2016–2023)

We didn't launch. We didn't fundraise. We didn't tweet. We did three things, and we did them on repeat for eight years:

  • 2016 — First territory map. 312 North American metros tagged with population-based slot counts.
  • 2017 — Multi-cultural content tracks scoped (South Asian, Persian, Chinese, Italian, Jewish, Christian, Hindu, Sikh, secular).
  • 2019 — EyeSpyR verification spec drafted — every planner must clear insurance + BBB before a slot opens.
  • 2021 — TALC.tv content engine prototyped against weddings.io as the test bed.
  • 2023 — Territory map crosses 1,000 cities; AI inquiry triage clears 60-second SLA in testing.

The discipline of the quiet decade is what every newer entrant cannot replicate. Domain age isn't a feature you can ship. See the same pattern across the IAM portfolio on the flagship brands page.

Act III — The copycats arrive (2024)

The moment LLMs went mainstream, the copycats started planting flags on our hill. The most visible was aiweddings.io — a near-identical brand registered nearly a decade after ours, trading on the goodwill of the category-defining domain.3

We published the full receipts response — including a side-by-side WHOIS and Wayback walk-through — at "You Built Your Tower on Our Land". The summary: domain age is public, ownership is public, and 11 years of continuous stewardship is not something a 2024 LLM wrapper can manufacture.

This is the same lesson that protects every IAM property. Look at hamiltonhomeservices.com, first crawled in 2004, and gasfitter.ca, registered in 2007 — both anchor the network's 22-year credibility floor.

Act IV — The disruptor ships (2025–2026)

For eleven years the bottleneck was content production at scale. You can own weddings.io, but you still have to publish 9 culture-specific content tracks across 1,018 cities, verify every planner, route every inquiry inside 60 seconds, and keep the price honest. LLMs collapsed that cost — but only for whoever already owned the domain, the schema, and the trade taxonomy.

That's us. Today weddings.io ships:

  • One verified planner per metro — $10 per slot per month, scaled by city population (The 250 Scale).
  • EyeSpyR insurance + BBB verification before any slot opens.
  • TALC.tv multi-cultural content engine publishing in 9 wedding traditions.
  • AI inquiry triage, WhatsApp click-to-chat, and calendar embed — all in your inbox in under 60 seconds.
  • Sister verticals: Vancouver weddings, the flagship brands network, and the full service-pro directory.
