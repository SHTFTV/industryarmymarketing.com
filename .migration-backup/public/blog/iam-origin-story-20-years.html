The IAM Origin Story: 20 Years of Building the Idea AI Finally Lets Us Finish

We didn't show up in 2024 with a fresh logo and an OpenAI key. Industry Army Marketing has been quietly assembling category domains, trade SEO, and local-market expertise since 1 2004 — back when "AI for small business" meant a spell-checker. This is the receipts-backed story of how we got here.

2004: One site, one trade, one city

The first stake in the ground was hamiltonhomeservices.com — a local directory for Hamilton, Ontario tradespeople. The Internet Archive's first crawl is dated March 24, 2004, and the site has been re-crawled 39 times since.2 That's the kind of continuity Google's algorithm rewards and that newer competitors literally cannot manufacture.

2007: The category-domain bet

Three years in, we made the bet that defined everything after: own the noun, not the brand. We started picking up exact-match category domains while they were still affordable. gasfitter.ca was registered on November 2, 2007 and has been held continuously ever since.3

"You can buy a billboard for a month. You can't buy 18 years of domain age."

2013–2015: Going national, going .io

By 2013 the playbook was working and we started expanding across verticals. weddings.io was first crawled by the Internet Archive on May 17, 2013 — 78 captures later it is still in our hands.4 The .io TLD was a deliberate choice: short, brandable, and at the time mostly uncontested by U.S. competitors.

2016–2023: The quiet decade

We didn't blog. We didn't tweet. We built. Across this stretch the network grew to include flagship category domains and tech plays that now anchor the army:

  • 2016 — kongtractors.com contractor directory goes live
  • 2018 — plowwow.com launches as the snow-removal vertical
  • 2019 — EyeSpyR.com marketing-intel tech spun out
  • 2021 — Talc.tv added to the tech stack
  • 2022 — Errands.io and Backhaul.io claimed for the transportation play
  • 2023 — promows.ca joins as the Canadian landscaping anchor

Read more about the network on our flagship brands page or browse the live service-professional verticals.

2024–2026: The idea AI finally lets us finish

For 20 years the bottleneck was content production. You can own gasfitter.ca, but you still have to write 500 trade-specific landing pages, keep them fresh, and rank them locally. LLMs collapsed that cost to near-zero — but only if you already had the domains, the schema discipline, and the local-trade taxonomy. We did.

Today the IAM network publishes more than 6,000 indexed pages, ships a flat-priced $10 slot for any service pro, and runs a free AI SEO scan built on the same 22-year category-domain backbone you see in the receipts above. The thesis hasn't changed since 2004 — own the noun, serve the trade, never rent your traffic. AI just lets us finish the job.
